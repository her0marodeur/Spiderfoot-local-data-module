*IT, DE, BE, NL, ES, CH, AU, CA, CO*

### ATM jackpotting and black-box attack crews

`EU-CLU-JACKPOT` · activity cluster · profit · degraded, capability high · since 2013

*DE, ES, IT, FR, GB, PL, MX, US*

**Aka:** black box crews, logical attack crews

Crews that open the ATM top box, disconnect the dispenser from the core, attach an unauthorised controller (black box) or install malware, and command a full cash-out. This is the archetypal hybrid attack: physical access is the enabler, software is the payload. European incidence has fallen sharply with dispenser-authentication and hard-disk encryption rollouts, but the technique persists against legacy estates and re-emerges wherever an operator defers firmware upgrades.

**Why it matters.** Directly demonstrates why ATM physical security and endpoint security cannot be governed separately. A successful jackpot empties every cassette without any building damage, so it is often detected only at reconciliation.

**Targets:** atm estate.

**Confidence:** E high · A low · F high.

> Attribution basis: Toolkits circulate commercially; crews are transient.

### Arson-for-profit and serial insurance fire rings

`GLB-CLU-ARSONFORPROFIT` · activity cluster · profit · active, capability low · since 1970

*US, CA, GB, IT, ES, AU, MY*

**Aka:** binge burners, fraud fires, torch rings

Rings that repeatedly set fire to insured residential and commercial property to collect payouts, often over many years and many properties before detection. Prosecutions consistently reveal serial rather than one-off behaviour: a Southern District of Illinois ring ran from 2014 to 2023 before its leader received 15 years, and an Ohio scheme involved at least six fires and more than USD 2.3 million in claims between 2013 and 2019. Property is frequently acquired cheaply, over-insured, and then burned, sometimes with paid third-party torches.

**Why it matters.** Arson is the insurance fraud typology that most reliably kills people. Fires set in terraced, multi-occupancy or commercially adjoined property endanger occupants, neighbours and responding firefighters, and the fraudster has no control over the outcome once the fire is set. For insurers the exposure is both the claim and the liability, and the detection signal is almost always the pattern across the book rather than anything visible on a single claim. Underwriting is the effective control point, since over-insurance relative to purchase price is the recurring precursor.

**On the record**

- *2025, Southern District of Illinois, US* — Ring leader sentenced to 15 years in federal prison for a string of arsons and insurance fraud running from 2014 to 2023.
- *2026, Northern District of Ohio, US* — Couple sentenced over a scheme involving at least six fires and more than USD 2.3 million in fraudulent claims between 2013 and 2019.

**Targets:** insured assets, corporate premises, claims field staff.

**Confidence:** E high · A moderate · F high.

> Attribution basis: Named defendants convicted in specific rings; the wider pattern is dispersed.

**Sources**

### Cable landing station and terrestrial fibre chokepoint exposure

`GLB-CLU-CABLELANDING` · activity cluster · hybrid · active, capability moderate · since 2010

*GB, FR, IE, PT, ES, EG, SG, US, AU, MY, TH*

**Aka:** landing station attacks, fibre chokepoints, cable cuts

The shore end of the subsea cable system: landing stations, beach manholes, terrestrial backhaul and the small number of geographic chokepoints where many cables converge. More than 150 cable faults occur worldwide each year, with fishing and anchoring accounting for close to 70 percent, so deliberate sabotage is a small minority of a large number. That statistic cuts both ways. It means most outages are accidental, and it means a deliberate cut is easy to hide inside the noise. Landing stations are a far softer target than the cable itself: they are on land, few in number, and often lightly guarded.

**Why it matters.** Financial messaging, market data, inter-datacentre replication and cloud connectivity all depend on this infrastructure, and route diversity purchased from carriers frequently converges at the same landing station or the same duct without the buying institution knowing. That is the actual risk: not that a cable will be cut, but that an institution's documented diversity is fictional at the shore end. Repair timelines are governed by cable ship availability, which is a global constraint no customer can escalate.

**Targets:** cable landing station, telecom subsea cable, data centre.

**Confidence:** E high · A low · F low.

> Attribution basis: Accidental and deliberate damage are difficult to distinguish; deliberate cases are rarely attributed.

### Card- and cash-trapping crews

`EU-CLU-TRAPPING` · activity cluster · profit · active, capability low · since 1998

*GB, FR, DE, IT, ES, BE, NL, PL*

**Aka:** Lebanese loop crews, cash claw crews

Low-technology crews that physically trap either the customer's card or the dispensed cash inside the ATM, then recover it after the customer leaves. Cash trapping is now the single highest-volume ATM attack type in Europe by incident count. Operators frequently work in pairs, one to fit the device and shoulder-surf the PIN, one to 'help' the confused customer and steal the card.

**Why it matters.** Cash trapping alone exceeded 10,500 incidents across Europe in the most recent EAST reporting year, making it the most common physical interference with the ATM estate. Losses per event are small but the volume, the direct customer harm, and the complaint handling burden are significant.

**On the record**

- *2025, EU-wide* — EAST reported 10,574 cash-trapping incidents (up 8 percent) and 2,500 card-trapping incidents (down 8 percent).

**Targets:** atm estate, customers.

**Confidence:** E high · A low · F high.

> Attribution basis: Predominantly itinerant small crews with no durable identity.

**Sources**

1. [EAST: Data relay attacks drive up European terminal related fraud incidents](https://www.association-secure-transactions.eu/data-relay-attacks-drive-up-european-terminal-related-fraud-incidents/) — industry

### Coerced-withdrawal and ATM duress cluster

`GLB-CLU-DURESSATM` · activity cluster · profit · active, capability low · since 1995

*BR, MX, CO, VE, AR, ZA, GB, FR, TH, MY*

**Aka:** forced withdrawal robbery, express kidnapping

Offenders who abduct or restrain a victim and drive them between ATMs, forcing withdrawals up to daily limits, sometimes across a rollover at midnight to double the take. Known as express kidnapping in Latin America, it also appears in European night-time economy areas as a short-duration variant following a street robbery.

**Why it matters.** The clearest case where transaction monitoring can detect an in-progress violent crime rather than just a loss. Repeated maximum withdrawals across multiple machines in a short window, especially spanning midnight, is a recognisable and actionable signature. Some institutions have implemented duress-aware limits and referral rules on this basis.

**Targets:** customers, atm estate.

**Confidence:** E high · A low · F high.

> Attribution basis: Predominantly opportunistic offenders.

### Courier fraud gangs

`EU-CLU-COURIERFRAUD` · activity cluster · profit · active, capability moderate · since 2010

*GB, IE, NL, DE, FR*

**Aka:** bank runner scams, police impersonation courier crews

Crews that telephone elderly victims posing as police or bank fraud teams, persuade them that their account is compromised, and then dispatch a physical courier to collect cards, PINs, cash or gold from the doorstep. It is a hybrid by construction: the deception is remote, but the value extraction requires a person to attend an address, which is also the point at which the crews are most detectable. UK losses exceed GBP 21 million a year.

**Why it matters.** Places a criminal physically at the customer's door on the strength of an impersonated bank identity, and frequently walks the victim into a branch to make a withdrawal the branch has no obvious reason to refuse. It is therefore both a fraud problem and a branch-floor intervention problem, and the primary reason banking protocol schemes exist.

**On the record**

- *2025-03, Lancashire, GB* — Six offenders jailed for a combined 25+ years for defrauding 14 vulnerable elderly victims of over GBP 1 million, cold-calling from a fake call centre in Blackburn while impersonating police and bank security.
- *2025, GB* — Multiple further custodial sentences across Suffolk, Hampshire, Hertfordshire and Northamptonshire; national losses exceeding GBP 21 million in a year.

**Targets:** customers, bank branch, bank staff.

**Confidence:** E high · A low · F high.

> Attribution basis: Couriers are prosecuted; organisers frequently are not identified.

### FSB Centre 16 and TsNIIKhM industrial control system programmes

`GLB-STATE-RUICS` · state proxy program · state directed · active, capability advanced · since 2012

*RU, US, GB, DE, NO, SA, TR*

**Aka:** Berserk Bear, Dragonfly, Energetic Bear, Crouching Yeti, TRITON, Xenotime

Two Russian government programmes charged together by the US Department of Justice in March 2022. FSB Centre 16 conducted long-running intrusion campaigns against global energy sector targets including nuclear generation, using supply-chain compromise of ICS vendor software and watering-hole attacks. The Central Scientific Research Institute of Chemistry and Mechanics (TsNIIKhM) was charged over the TRITON attack on a petrochemical facility's Triconex safety instrumented system, which is the clearest documented case anywhere of a state programme reaching into the safety layer designed to prevent explosions and loss of life.

**Why it matters.** TRITON is the reference case for cyber operations that produce physical harm, and it matters here for the same reason as the grid records: financial services depend on energy and industrial infrastructure it neither owns nor can inspect. The specific lesson is about safety systems. TRITON did not target production, it targeted the system that stops production killing people, which is the analytical equivalent of attacking a bank's controls rather than its money. Exposure is entirely indirect and should be treated as a continuity and concentration problem.

**On the record**

- *2017, Middle East* — TRITON malware deployed against a petrochemical plant's Triconex safety instrumented system, the safety layer intended to prevent catastrophic release.
- *2022-03, US* — Four Russian government employees charged over two historical hacking campaigns targeting critical infrastructure worldwide, including energy sector intrusions and the TRITON attack.

**Targets:** power grid infrastructure, data centre, corporate premises, hardware supply chain.

**Confidence:** E high · A high · F low · S high.

### Hardware and firmware supply-chain tampering cluster

`GLB-CLU-HWIMPLANT` · activity cluster · hybrid · active, capability high · since 2008

*US, GB, DE, NL, SG, CN, MY, TH, AU, BR*

**Aka:** hardware additions, T1200, implanted devices, interdiction

Physical modification of hardware or firmware somewhere between manufacture and deployment: implanted devices added to servers, network gear, ATMs, HSMs and payment terminals; firmware flashed during manufacture, repair or shipping interdiction; or rogue devices attached to a network by someone who walked in. This is the category where public claims and public evidence diverge most sharply. The 2018 Bloomberg Supermicro report remains unverified and denied by the named parties, and it merged a hardware claim, an actor claim and a tasking claim into a single assertion, which is precisely the failure mode this database's per-variable confidence model exists to avoid. Firmware compromise and rogue device addition, by contrast, are routinely demonstrated.

**Why it matters.** Banks operate more security-critical hardware than almost any other commercial sector: HSMs, PIN pads, ATM dispensers and cash controllers, card personalisation equipment. Each has a supply chain, a repair loop and a decommissioning path, and each is handled by third parties. The realistic and demonstrated threats are firmware tampering during repair, terminal substitution in the field and rogue devices added inside offices. The exotic nation-state implant scenario absorbs disproportionate attention relative to its evidence base, and treating both at the same confidence level distorts spending.

**Targets:** hardware supply chain, atm estate, pos terminals, data centre, insiders.

**Confidence:** E moderate · A low · F moderate · S low.

> Attribution basis: Very few incidents are attributed to a named actor; the most publicised claim remains unverified and denied.

`GLB-CLU-SECINDUSTRY` · activity cluster · profit · active, capability moderate · since 1995

*BR, MX, ZA, IT, FR, GB, MY, TH, AU*

Deliberate placement or recruitment of personnel inside guarding companies, cash-in-transit operators and ATM service providers. Because these firms compete on price, vetting quality is uneven and turnover is high, and they hold precisely the schedule, route and value information that makes a high-value physical attack feasible. ### Targeted violence against financial sector executives and staff

`GLB-CLU-EXECTARGET` · activity cluster · hybrid · active, capability moderate · since 1990

*US, MX, BR, CO, HT, NL, SE, FR, GB, EC, PE*

**Aka:** executive targeting, banker kidnapping

A convergent pattern rather than a single actor: organised crime coercing compliance and investigations staff, ideologically motivated attacks on named executives, grievance-driven lone actors, and kidnapping of banking personnel for ransom in high-kidnap jurisdictions. The common feature is that the individual, not the institution, is the attack surface, and that home addresses and family details are the targeting data.

**Why it matters.** Executive and staff protection has moved from a niche concern to a core financial-sector security discipline, driven by three developments: cheap brokered violence in the Nordics and Australia, doxxing and open-source targeting, and the willingness of networks like the Mocro Maffia to attack officers of the justice system. Data protection of staff personal information is now a physical security control, not just a privacy one.

**Targets:** bank staff, executives hnwi, insiders.

**Confidence:** E high · A low · F moderate.

### Vessel scuttling and marine insurance fraud syndicates

`GLB-CLU-MARINEFRAUD` · activity cluster · profit · active, capability moderate · since 1970

*GR, TR, AE, SG, MY, TH, PA, LR, CY*

**Aka:** scuttling, rust bucket fraud, phantom cargo fraud

Deliberate sinking or wrecking of over-insured vessels, frequently combined with claims for high-value cargo that never existed. The economics are counter-cyclical: scuttling clusters during downturns in the shipping market, when an ageing vessel is worth more insured than trading. Recent cases carry a combined potential claims value approaching USD 100 million, and in the Atlantik Confidence matter insurers paid USD 22 million on a claim later found to be a deliberate act and could not recover it. Proof is exceptionally hard because the evidence sinks with the ship.

**Why it matters.** The insurance loss category where the fraudulent act is itself a large-scale physical event with crew lives at risk, and where the insurer must prove deliberate action to a high evidential standard against a defendant who has destroyed the evidence. It is directly relevant to marine hull, cargo and P&I underwriting, and to the trade finance banks lending against the same cargo, since phantom cargo fraud frequently defrauds both simultaneously.

**On the record**

- *2013, Indian Ocean* — Atlantik Confidence sank; insurers paid USD 22 million on a claim subsequently found to be a deliberate act, without recovery.

**Targets:** insured assets, armoured air cargo, corporate premises.

**Confidence:** E high · A low · F high.

> Attribution basis: Ownership is deliberately obscured; deliberate loss is proven in only a small minority of suspected cases.
