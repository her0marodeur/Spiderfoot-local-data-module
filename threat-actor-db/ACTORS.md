### 'Ndrangheta

`EU-OCG-NDRANGHETA`

**Also known as:** Calabrian mafia

**Class:** network &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Motivation:** profit &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Status:** active &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Capability:** advanced

**Active since:** 1860

**Operates in:** IT, DE, BE, NL, ES, CH, AU, CA, CO

**Categories:** money laundering physical, extortion protection, insider recruitment, staff targeting

**Targets:** corporate premises, insiders, bank staff, money service business

Europe's most financially powerful mafia organisation, structured around blood-tied 'ndrine, dominant in cocaine importation and deeply embedded in legitimate business across northern Italy, Germany, the Benelux and Australia. Its distinguishing characteristic for financial institutions is not violence but penetration: it acquires and operates real businesses, obtains credit, wins public contracts and corrupts professionals.

**Why the financial sector should care**

The principal European source of sophisticated laundering through apparently legitimate corporate customers, particularly in construction, hospitality, logistics and waste. Physical risk to bank staff is low but non-zero, and manifests as pressure on branch managers and professional intermediaries in affected regions. The main exposure is credit and AML rather than security.

**Tactics, techniques and procedures**

- Acquisition and control of legitimate companies to obtain banking relationships and credit
- Corruption of professionals, local officials and, occasionally, bank staff
- Extortion of businesses in areas of territorial control
- Trade-based laundering through logistics, construction and hospitality
- Selective intimidation rather than demonstrative violence in northern European operations

| attribution | high | Structure judicially established. |

**Watch indicators**

- Corporate customers in high-risk sectors with rapid unexplained growth in Calabrian-linked ownership networks
- Repeated changes of nominal directorship with unchanged operational control
- Branch staff reporting pressure to expedite lending decisions

### ATM jackpotting and black-box attack crews

`EU-CLU-JACKPOT`

**Also known as:** black box crews, logical attack crews

**Class:** activity cluster &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Motivation:** profit &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Status:** degraded &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Capability:** high

**Active since:** 2013

**Operates in:** DE, ES, IT, FR, GB, PL, MX, US

**Categories:** atm logical physical attack

**Targets:** atm estate

Crews that open the ATM top box, disconnect the dispenser from the core, attach an unauthorised controller (black box) or install malware, and command a full cash-out. This is the archetypal hybrid attack: physical access is the enabler, software is the payload. European incidence has fallen sharply with dispenser-authentication and hard-disk encryption rollouts, but the technique persists against legacy estates and re-emerges wherever an operator defers firmware upgrades.

**Why the financial sector should care**

Directly demonstrates why ATM physical security and endpoint security cannot be governed separately. A successful jackpot empties every cassette without any building damage, so it is often detected only at reconciliation.

**Tactics, techniques and procedures**

- Forced or key-based opening of the ATM upper cabinet
- Disconnection of the dispenser and attachment of an unauthorised controller
- Deployment of dispenser-targeting malware via USB or exposed ports
- Insider or ex-insider provision of service keys and cabinet access codes
- Use of money mules to collect dispensed cash within a short window

**Confidence (assessed per variable)**

| Variable | Confidence | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| existence | high | Long-running EAST reporting and vendor advisories. |
| attribution | low | Toolkits circulate commercially; crews are transient. |
| financial targeting | high | ATM cash-out is the sole objective. |

**Watch indicators**

- Top-box open alarms outside scheduled maintenance windows
- Unexplained dispenser resets or firmware version mismatches
- Cash-out events with no corresponding authorised transaction record

**Related records:** `EU-CLU-INSIDER`

### Card- and cash-trapping crews

`EU-CLU-TRAPPING`

**Also known as:** Lebanese loop crews, cash claw crews

**Class:** activity cluster &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Motivation:** profit &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Status:** active &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Capability:** low

**Active since:** 1998

**Operates in:** GB, FR, DE, IT, ES, BE, NL, PL

**Categories:** card skimming tampering, customer predation

**Targets:** atm estate, customers

Low-technology crews that physically trap either the customer's card or the dispensed cash inside the ATM, then recover it after the customer leaves. Cash trapping is now the single highest-volume ATM attack type in Europe by incident count. Operators frequently work in pairs, one to fit the device and shoulder-surf the PIN, one to 'help' the confused customer and steal the card.

**Why the financial sector should care**

Cash trapping alone exceeded 10,500 incidents across Europe in the most recent EAST reporting year, making it the most common physical interference with the ATM estate. Losses per event are small but the volume, the direct customer harm, and the complaint handling burden are significant.

**Tactics, techniques and procedures**

- Insertion of a claw, comb or adhesive strip into the cash dispenser to retain notes
- Lebanese loop and similar retention devices in the card slot
- Social engineering of the victim at the machine, posing as a helpful bystander
- Shoulder surfing or overlay keypads to capture the PIN before the card is trapped
- Rapid recovery of the trapped card or cash within minutes of the victim leaving

**Confidence (assessed per variable)**

| Variable | Confidence | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| existence | high | EAST incident counts and routine national prosecutions. |
| attribution | low | Predominantly itinerant small crews with no durable identity. |
| financial targeting | high | Attacks are performed only on payment terminals. |

**Notable incidents**

- **2025** (EU-wide) — EAST reported 10,574 cash-trapping incidents (up 8 percent) and 2,500 card-trapping incidents (down 8 percent).

**Watch indicators**

- Spike in 'cash not dispensed' disputes concentrated on individual machines
- Repeat customer reports of a helpful stranger present at the machine
- Foreign objects recovered during first-line maintenance

**Related records:** `EU-CLU-SKIM-RO`

### Coerced and recruited insider cluster (banking, CIT and ATM services)

`EU-CLU-INSIDER`

**Also known as:** insider recruitment, corrupted staff

**Class:** activity cluster &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Motivation:** profit &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Status:** active &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Capability:** moderate

**Active since:** 2000

**Operates in:** NL, BE, GB, IE, DE, ES, IT, FR

**Categories:** insider recruitment, staff targeting

**Targets:** insiders, bank staff, cash centre, cit vehicle, atm estate

Not a group but a persistent enabling pattern: organised crime networks recruit or coerce employees of banks, cash-in-transit operators, ATM service providers and their contractors to supply schedules, keys, alarm states, customer data and access. Dutch and Belgian port corruption is the best-documented analogue, and the same recruitment playbook, social approach, small first favour, escalation, threat, is applied to financial operations staff.

**Why the financial sector should care**

Almost every high-value physical attack in this database that succeeded against a hardened target involved insider information. Insider recruitment is therefore the single highest-leverage control point available to a financial institution, and the one most often owned by HR and vetting rather than security.

**Tactics, techniques and procedures**

- Social approach through family, community or debt relationships
- Escalating requests beginning with low-consequence information
- Coercion through gambling or drug debt, or threats to family
- Recruitment of contractors and agency staff who fall outside core vetting
- Payment through cash, cryptocurrency or debt forgiveness rather than bank transfer

**Confidence (assessed per variable)**

| Variable | Confidence | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| existence | high | Recurring feature of European CIT, ATM and port prosecutions. |
| attribution | low | Insiders are attributable; the recruiting network frequently is not. |
| financial targeting | high | Recruitment is directed at staff with access to cash, keys and schedules. |

**Watch indicators**

- Unexplained affluence or sudden debt resolution in cash-access roles
- Access to schedules or CCTV outside role requirements
- Contractor staff churn without corresponding vetting records
- Employee reporting of external approaches, which is the control that actually works

**Related records:** `EU-OCG-MOCRO`, `EU-CLU-CIT-IT`, `EU-CLU-JACKPOT`, `AM-CLU-ATMTECH`

**Targets:** pos terminals, atm estate, customers

The fastest-growing physical-adjacent attack on European payment terminals. Compromised card credentials are provisioned into a wallet on an operator-controlled handset; a mule then presents that handset at a genuine terminal, with relay software bridging the NFC exchange to a device held elsewhere. It converts remote credential theft into card-present transactions at physical merchants, which is why it belongs in a physical threat model rather than a purely cyber one.

**Why the financial sector should care**

**Tactics, techniques and procedures**

- Provisioning of phished or malware-harvested card data into attacker-controlled mobile wallets
- NFC relay malware bridging a victim device and a mule handset in real time
- Recruitment of low-level mules to present handsets at high-value merchants
- Cash-out through contactless ATM withdrawal and resaleable high-value goods
- Geographic dispersal of taps to stay under velocity thresholds

**Notable incidents**

### Disaster, unrest and outage exploitation cluster

`GLB-CLU-DISASTERLOOT`

**Also known as:** riot looting of branches, blackout ATM attacks

**Class:** activity cluster &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Motivation:** opportunistic &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Status:** active &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Capability:** low

**Active since:** 1990

**Operates in:** ZA, CL, EC, FR, GB, US, BR, HT, TH

**Categories:** branch robbery, atm physical attack

**Targets:** bank branch, atm estate, cash centre

Exploitation of civil unrest, natural disaster or extended power and telecoms outage to attack branches and ATMs while police and alarm response are saturated or unavailable. It is opportunistic rather than organised, but the losses are concentrated: a single unrest event can destroy dozens of machines and multiple branches in a night.

**Why the financial sector should care**

The scenario in which every response-dependent control fails simultaneously. Alarm monitoring, guard response and police attendance all assume ordinary conditions. Planning for it means accepting that during the event nothing can be protected, and concentrating on cash withdrawal from the estate beforehand and rapid restoration afterwards.

**Tactics, techniques and procedures**

- Attack on branches and ATMs during unrest when response is saturated
- Exploitation of extended power or telecoms outage disabling alarms
- Forced entry through fire-damaged or already-breached premises
- Secondary looting of adjacent commercial premises

**Confidence (assessed per variable)**

| Variable | Confidence | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| existence | high | Repeatedly documented during major unrest events internationally. |
| attribution | low | Opportunistic and largely anonymous. |
| financial targeting | moderate | Branches and ATMs are attacked as available high-value targets, not selected in advance. |

**Watch indicators**

- Civil unrest forecasts and protest scheduling in branch catchments
- Extended grid or telecoms outage affecting alarm reporting
- Attacks on adjacent commercial premises as an escalation signal

**Related records:** `EU-CLU-METALCABLE`, `AM-GRP-VIVANSANM`

### POS terminal swap and merchant-side tampering cluster

`GLB-CLU-POSSWAP`

**Also known as:** terminal swap crews, merchant terminal tampering

**Class:** activity cluster &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Motivation:** profit &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Status:** active &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Capability:** moderate

**Active since:** 2008

**Operates in:** GB, FR, DE, ES, US, AU, MY, TH, BR

**Categories:** card skimming tampering, insider recruitment

**Targets:** pos terminals, insiders, customers

Crews that substitute a merchant's payment terminal with a tampered unit, or open and modify a terminal in situ, to harvest card data at the point of acceptance. Access is obtained either by posing as maintenance engineers or by recruiting merchant staff. It is quieter and higher yield than ATM skimming because compromise can persist for months without any customer-visible symptom.

**Why the financial sector should care**

Directly attacks the acquirer's estate and the merchant relationship, and it is frequently discovered only through common-point-of-purchase analysis rather than physical inspection. Acquirers carry the reconciliation, remediation and merchant remediation cost, and the insider dimension means terminal logistics and engineer identity verification are the real controls.

**Tactics, techniques and procedures**

- Impersonation of terminal maintenance engineers to gain physical access
- Substitution of a genuine terminal with a pre-modified unit
- In-situ opening and implanting of a data-capture module
- Recruitment or bribery of merchant staff to permit or ignore access
- Interception of terminals in the distribution and repair supply chain
- Periodic revisits to harvest stored data

**Confidence (assessed per variable)**

| Variable | Confidence | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| existence | high | Long-running acquirer and law enforcement casework internationally. |
| attribution | low | Perpetrators are diverse and rarely form persistent groups. |
| financial targeting | high | Payment acceptance infrastructure is the sole target. |

**Watch indicators**

- Terminal serial numbers not matching deployment records
- Unscheduled engineer visits reported by merchants
- Common-point-of-purchase concentration at a single merchant
- Tamper-evident seal discrepancies at routine servicing

**Related records:** `EU-CLU-SKIM-RO`, `AM-CLU-EBTSKIM`, `EU-CLU-INSIDER`

**Sources**

- [EAST: Data relay attacks drive up European terminal related fraud incidents](https://www.association-secure-transactions.eu/data-relay-attacks-drive-up-european-terminal-related-fraud-incidents/) — *industry*

### Private security and cash-logistics industry infiltration cluster

`GLB-CLU-SECINDUSTRY`

**Also known as:** guard force infiltration, CIT insider compromise

**Class:** activity cluster &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Motivation:** profit &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Status:** active &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Capability:** moderate

**Active since:** 1995

**Operates in:** BR, MX, ZA, IT, FR, GB, MY, TH, AU

**Categories:** insider recruitment, cash in transit attack, cash centre attack

**Targets:** insiders, cit vehicle, cash centre, atm estate

Deliberate placement or recruitment of personnel inside guarding companies, cash-in-transit operators and ATM service providers. Because these firms compete on price, vetting quality is uneven and turnover is high, and they hold precisely the schedule, route and value information that makes a high-value physical attack feasible. Almost every large CIT and cash centre loss in this database has an insider component.

**Why the financial sector should care**

Financial institutions outsource the function but retain the loss. The practical implication is that vetting standards, access segregation and information compartmentation at the contractor are a bank control problem, and contractually specifying them is more effective than any physical hardening the bank can buy.

**Tactics, techniques and procedures**

- Placement of associates into guarding and CIT roles through legitimate recruitment
- Recruitment of existing staff through debt, community ties or coercion
- Extraction of route timings, load values, alarm states and key control procedures
- Facilitation of access during an attack, including disabling of controls
- Exploitation of high turnover and subcontracting chains to defeat vetting

**Confidence (assessed per variable)**

| Variable | Confidence | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| existence | high | Recurring feature of major CIT and cash centre prosecutions internationally. |
| attribution | low | Insiders identified case by case; recruiting networks often not. |
| financial targeting | high | Recruitment targets roles with access to cash, keys and schedules. |

**Watch indicators**

- Contractor staff churn without corresponding vetting evidence
- Access to schedules or CCTV beyond role requirements
- Unexplained affluence or debt resolution among cash-access staff
- Subcontracting chains that obscure who actually holds the keys

**Related records:** `EU-CLU-INSIDER`, `EU-CLU-CIT-IT`, `AM-CLU-USARMORED`, `AM-CLU-BULLIONCARGO`

### Synthetic-media-enabled physical intrusion and courier fraud cluster

`GLB-CLU-DEEPFAKEPHYS`

**Also known as:** deepfake-enabled impersonation, voice clone authorisation

**Class:** activity cluster &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Motivation:** profit &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Status:** active &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Capability:** moderate

**Active since:** 2023

**Operates in:** GB, US, AE, HK, SG, AU, MY

**Categories:** fraud enabled physical, insider recruitment, customer predation

**Targets:** customers, insiders, bank staff, corporate premises

Use of cloned voices and synthetic video to obtain physical outcomes: authorising a courier collection, instructing a facilities team to admit a contractor, approving an out-of-band cash release, or persuading a customer to hand over value at the door. The important shift is that synthetic media is increasingly used to move a physical process rather than to complete a purely digital transaction.

**Why the financial sector should care**

It undermines voice and video as verification for exactly the processes that were deliberately moved off digital channels for safety, including out-of-band callbacks for high-value payments and physical access authorisation. Institutions that responded to payment fraud by adding a human voice check have, in some cases, added an attack surface rather than removed one.

**Tactics, techniques and procedures**

- Voice cloning of executives to authorise urgent payments or access
- Synthetic video in conference calls to legitimise instructions
- Impersonation of bank staff to direct customers to hand over cards or cash
- Manufactured urgency to defeat callback and cooling-off controls
- Combination with courier collection to complete extraction physically

**Confidence (assessed per variable)**

| Variable | Confidence | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| existence | high | Multiple confirmed corporate incidents and law enforcement warnings. |
| attribution | low | Perpetrators are usually offshore and unidentified. |
| financial targeting | high | Payment authorisation and bank identity are the targets. |

**Watch indicators**

- Out-of-band authorisation requests originating from an unfamiliar number
- Urgent instructions that discourage independent verification
- Facilities or courier access authorised verbally without written confirmation
- Customers reporting convincing calls from apparent bank staff

**Related records:** `EU-CLU-COURIERFRAUD`, `APAC-CLU-MACAUSCAM`, `APAC-CLU-VIRTKIDNAP`

### Targeted violence against financial sector executives and staff

`GLB-CLU-EXECTARGET`

**Also known as:** executive targeting, banker kidnapping

**Class:** activity cluster &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Motivation:** hybrid &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Status:** active &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Capability:** moderate

**Active since:** 1990

**Operates in:** US, MX, BR, CO, HT, NL, SE, FR, GB, EC, PE

**Categories:** staff targeting, kidnap for ransom

**Targets:** bank staff, executives hnwi, insiders

A convergent pattern rather than a single actor: organised crime coercing compliance and investigations staff, ideologically motivated attacks on named executives, grievance-driven lone actors, and kidnapping of banking personnel for ransom in high-kidnap jurisdictions. The common feature is that the individual, not the institution, is the attack surface, and that home addresses and family details are the targeting data.

**Why the financial sector should care**

Executive and staff protection has moved from a niche concern to a core financial-sector security discipline, driven by three developments: cheap brokered violence in the Nordics and Australia, doxxing and open-source targeting, and the willingness of networks like the Mocro Maffia to attack officers of the justice system. Data protection of staff personal information is now a physical security control, not just a privacy one.

**Tactics, techniques and procedures**

- Open-source and breach-derived collection of home addresses, vehicles and routines
- Surveillance of residences and predictable movements
- Commissioned assault, arson or shooting via intermediaries
- Kidnapping for ransom of executives and their families in high-risk jurisdictions
- Threats and harassment against compliance, collections and foreclosure staff
- Coercion to obtain access, information or favourable decisions

**Confidence (assessed per variable)**

| Variable | Confidence | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| existence | high | Multiple documented cases across the jurisdictions listed. |
| attribution | low | Perpetrators range from state-linked to organised crime to lone actors; brokered violence deliberately obscures the commissioner. |
| financial targeting | moderate | Financial sector individuals are one of several professional target groups, alongside lawyers, journalists and officials. |

**Notable incidents**

- **2019-09-18** (Amsterdam, NL) — Killing of lawyer Derk Wiersum demonstrated Dutch organised crime's willingness to attack officers of the justice system, a precedent directly relevant to financial crime investigators.

**Watch indicators**

- Staff personal data appearing in breach dumps or on hostile forums
- Employee reports of surveillance, unexpected deliveries or approaches
- Escalating grievance correspondence naming individual staff
- Institution named in an active hostile campaign

**Related records:** `EU-OCG-MOCRO`, `EU-CLU-VIOLENCESERV`, `EU-CLU-TIGERKIDNAP`, `AM-CLU-USDOMEXT`
